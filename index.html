What I work on

The common thread: turning the activity traces open source leaves behind into evidence people can act on.

01Project health

Is a project thriving, stagnating or quietly dying? Maintainer load, responsiveness, release cadence and community dynamics as a measurable layer.

02OSS risk

Health as the missing piece of supply-chain risk management — beyond licenses and CVEs, towards dependency resilience and compliance.

03Data mining

Large-scale analysis of software repositories, from GrimoireLab and CHAOSS tooling to the Software Heritage archive.

04Metrics & models

Metrics that matter for OSPOs, InnerSource programs and maintainers — and how to avoid the ones that don't.

05Impact measurement

Showing the value of open source investment and public funding, and how public institutions contribute to code as a digital public good.

06Geopolitics of code

Digital sovereignty, cross-border collaboration and fragmentation — read through data rather than rhetoric.

About

I started as a researcher at Universidad Rey Juan Carlos, taking part in EU-funded projects on open source metrics, and earned a PhD in 2012 analysing developer activity patterns and bugs in the Mozilla community.

I co-founded Bitergia, which spent over a decade providing software development analytics for open source and InnerSource ecosystems, and later led it as CEO. Along the way I served on the CHAOSS governing board and helped build the InnerSource Commons Foundation.

Today I focus on open source intelligence at scale: most recently co-developing the methodology behind The State of Public Code with Software Heritage, UNESCO and UN-ODET — an evidence-based look at how governments use and contribute to open source.
